Build a working mobile-first prototype called “MOAI BMU” for a Beach Management Unit official in coastal Kenya.

The official records fish landings, checks AI-assisted drafts, approves records and views useful summaries for their BMU. Prioritize this daily workflow. Make the app usable for field demonstrations on an Android phone.

## Product scope

Build these connected screens:

1. Today
2. Record a landing
3. Review and approve
4. My records
5. BMU summary

Use synthetic data throughout. Display a discreet “Prototype · Demo data” label. Do not build county dashboards, forecasting, payments, licensing, enforcement scoring or historical ledger digitisation in this version.

Use a demo official assigned to one BMU and landing site. Allow switching between clearly labelled demo sites through settings.

## Design

Design for a busy landing site, bright sunlight and intermittent connectivity.

- Large touch targets, readable text and strong contrast.
- Short steps with clear progress and minimal typing.
- Plain language and icons accompanied by text.
- English and Swahili language options. Keep translation text easy to review and update.
- Kilograms for weight, Kenyan shillings for prices and Africa/Nairobi for local time.
- Show connection and sync status clearly.
- Keep the main action, “Record a landing,” easy to reach.
- Use a calm, practical design with a restrained coastal colour palette.

## Today screen

Show:

- Official’s name, BMU and landing site.
- Approved landings recorded today.
- Records awaiting review.
- Records saved on this device awaiting sync.
- Recorded catch weight today.
- A prominent “Record a landing” button.

Distinguish saved, approved and synced states. Never imply that a locally saved record has reached another system.

## Record a landing

Offer three capture methods:

### A. Enter details

Provide a short guided form:

- Landing site, prefilled from the official’s assignment.
- Landing date and time, editable.
- Vessel code or name.
- Crew count, optional.
- Gear type, optional.
- Departure and return times, optional.
- Broad fishing area, optional.
- One or more catch items.

Each catch item has:

- Local fish name.
- Species or species group, if known.
- Weight in kg.
- Weight basis: measured or estimated.
- Price basis: KES per kg, total sale amount or unknown.
- Price amount when known.
- Optional photo.

Model one landing with multiple catch items. Do not count each species line as a separate boat landing. Support mixed or unidentified catch without forcing a false species identification.

Allow an optional observation, such as unusual catch or a market disruption. Keep it separate from measured catch data.

### B. Record voice

Let the official record, replay and delete a short voice note.

Also provide a “Try sample voice report” button using this synthetic example:

“Demo Boat 01 landed 12 kilograms of rabbitfish at Demo Landing A. Price was 350 Kenyan shillings per kilogram.”

Show the transcript and proposed fields before approval.

For the prepared demonstration, intentionally suggest 21 kg instead of 12 kg and highlight it for review. Label this as a seeded demonstration error, not a measured AI result.

### C. Take or upload a photo

Support camera capture and image upload.

Show the image alongside suggested species or species groups. The official must confirm or correct the suggestion.

Never infer weight, price, crew or fishing location from a fish photo.

Include a prepared demonstration image and suggestions. For arbitrary uploaded images, retain the image and allow manual entry unless a real image-analysis service is configured.

## AI behaviour

The prototype must work without paid AI credentials.

Provide clearly labelled prepared examples for demonstrating voice extraction and photo suggestions. Never present canned results as analysis of arbitrary user media.

Structure AI processing behind a replaceable service interface so real transcription and image analysis can be added later. If real processing is configured, keep credentials on the server.

AI creates drafts only. It must never approve records automatically.

Do not invent missing values. Show “Unknown” or “Needs confirmation.” Avoid numeric confidence scores unless they come from an actual, documented source.

## Review and approve

Show the source audio, transcript or photo beside editable fields.

Highlight uncertain or missing information. Let the official:

- Correct values.
- Add or remove catch items.
- Replay audio or inspect the image.
- Save a draft.
- Reject a suggestion.
- Approve the completed record.

For demonstration validation, require landing site, landing date/time, vessel identifier, a local catch name or category and positive weight. These are configurable prototype rules, not claims about Kenya’s official reporting requirements.

Allow unknown prices. Validate known prices against the selected price basis.

Keep drafts separate from approved records. Approval should record the official and timestamp.

After approval, show confirmation, sync status and a “Record another landing” action.

## Records and corrections

Provide filters for date, status and species.

Each record should show its landing details, catch items, source media and history.

Allow amendments to approved records with a reason. Preserve the previous values and record who changed them and when.

Use separate record IDs and sync status. Retrying a sync must not create duplicate landings.

## Offline behaviour

Use persistent browser storage, preferably IndexedDB, for drafts, records and captured media.

After the app has loaded once:

- Allow capture, editing and local approval without connectivity.
- Preserve records after refresh.
- Queue uploads and server-dependent processing until connectivity returns.
- Show “Saved on this phone,” “Waiting to sync” and “Synced” accurately.
- Keep source media until upload is acknowledged.
- Make sync failures recoverable without losing data.

Include a clearly labelled demo connectivity toggle. Distinguish simulated connectivity from actual network availability.

If server sync is simulated, explicitly label it. Do not claim real multi-device sync.

## BMU summary

Show a simple daily and weekly summary based only on approved records:

- Recorded landings.
- Recorded catch weight.
- Weight by species or local category.
- Observed prices where available.
- Recorded sale value where calculable.
- Records with missing prices.
- Pending reviews and unsynced records.

Calculate value consistently: weight × price per kg, or the entered total sale amount. Never treat missing prices as zero.

Explain that these are summaries of captured records, not estimates of the entire fishery.

Do not claim stock depletion, predict catches or recommend fishing grounds from these records.

Include a simple CSV download. Label it “Prototype export.” Do not claim compatibility with Kobo or national FIMS until their actual schemas and requirements have been confirmed.

## Demo data and walkthrough

Seed approximately 20 synthetic landings across a week, including:

- A landing with multiple catch items.
- An unknown species group.
- A missing price.
- A total sale price.
- An estimated weight.
- A record awaiting review.
- An approved record awaiting sync.
- A corrected approved record.

Include a “Reset demo” action.

Make this walkthrough work end to end:

Record sample voice report → inspect draft → correct 21 kg to 12 kg → approve → view history → see the BMU summary update.

The corrected example must contribute 12 kg and KES 4,200 to the summary, and count as one landing.

## Implementation and verification

Choose a simple stack that runs reliably in Replit. Build working interactions and persistence, not just visual screens.

Keep record storage, AI processing and sync logic separate so each can be replaced later.

Verify:

- Drafts survive refresh.
- Offline capture preserves entered data.
- AI suggestions require human approval.
- Drafts are excluded from summaries.
- Multiple catch items count as one landing.
- Unknown prices are handled correctly.
- Amendments preserve history.
- Sync retries do not duplicate records.

Finish with a short README explaining how to run the prototype, the demo walkthrough, which capabilities are real or simulated, and the limitations before field use.
